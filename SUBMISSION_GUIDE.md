# Submit your team project

**Deadline: Thursday, 12 November 2026, 11:59 p.m. EAT (20:59 UTC).**

One team member submits for all three members. The other members should check the package and video before submission.

## 1. Prepare your files

Finish the project in your existing team repository. Commit that version and copy its full commit SHA from GitHub's commit page. Keep the original repository and its history.

Complete the [team README template](templates/TEAM_README.md). Explain your idea and approach in short, clear paragraphs. Include **all required materials and a working video link**. The template lists what the judges need.

Download your project files or copy them from your computer. You need the contents of your project, not its hidden `.git` folder. Remove passwords, API keys, private data, virtual environments and caches. Keep folder structure intact.

## 2. Make your own copy of the submission repository

Open this central repository and click **Fork** at the top right. Choose your GitHub account, keep the repository name and the default `main` branch, then click **Create fork**. Only one fork is needed for your team.

You are now editing your own copy. Check that the page header starts with **your GitHub username**.

## 3. Open your assigned folder

In your fork, click **teams**, then your assigned folder:

- Team 1 uses `teams/team-01/`.
- Team 7 uses `teams/team-07/`.
- Team 24 uses `teams/team-24/`.

Keep all team files here. Do not change other folders or root files. Do not create a folder inside another team's folder.

## 4. Add your project and README

Inside your team folder, use **Add file → Upload files**. Drag your project files/folders into the upload area, add a commit message such as `Submit Team 07 project`, and commit to **your fork's main** branch.

Replace the team's placeholder `README.md` with your completed README. If you use the browser, click the file and the pencil icon to edit it, then commit the change. You can copy the template into the editor first. Do not upload a single ZIP in place of browsable source code.

For bigger projects, use Git on your computer to copy the project into your fork's assigned team folder, commit and push. Ask for help if the browser cannot upload your folder. Do not flatten your project's folders just to make the upload work.

Check the result: `teams/team-07/README.md`, `teams/team-07/app.py`, `teams/team-07/requirements.txt` and your own data/docs folders are examples of the correct location. Your actual filenames may differ.

## 5. Check the video and evidence

Your README must contain a link to a video that **presents your idea and demonstrates the solution**. Keep the explanation concise. The existing 2–3 minute recording is a suitable format; if you use separate presentation and demo videos, link both clearly.

Show the problem and language, your data contribution and approach, a working demo on new input, actual results and limitations, and the team's contributions. An unlisted YouTube link or a Drive video accessible to judges is suitable. Test the link from an account that does not own the video. Avoid a link that requires judges to request access. Do not upload raw video files into Git.

Check that the code, evaluation files and data links correspond to the version identified in your README. Explain access steps for restricted data using the agreed private channel; never publish private records to satisfy a checklist.

## 6. Open your pull request

Return to your fork's main page. Click **Contribute → Open pull request**. If that control is unavailable, use **Pull requests → New pull request → compare across forks**.

Check:

- **Base repository:** `OmondiKevin/sces-nlp-hub-datathon-2026`.
- **Base branch:** `main`.
- **Head repository:** your own fork.
- **Compare branch:** your fork's `main` (or the submission branch you actually used).

Use the title **Team 07 | Final submission**, changing the team number. Complete the supplied checklist. Under **Files changed**, confirm that every change is inside your own team folder, then click **Create pull request**. Submit a normal open PR, not a draft. Do not merge it yourself.

## 7. Confirm you have submitted

Open the PR and check that it says **Open**, has the correct team number and contains the expected files. Copy its URL and keep it as your receipt. The deadline concerns your open PR and submitted version, not when an organiser merges it.

If the validation check fails, open **Details**, read the message and correct the files in the same fork branch. Your PR updates automatically. If you are stuck, contact Kevin/Titus before the deadline with your team number, PR URL and the error.

Only submit one PR per team. Use that PR for corrections and clearly disclose any changes made after the deadline.
